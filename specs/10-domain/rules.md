# Rules

## Conventions
- **Mod** means Ctrl on Windows/Linux and Cmd on macOS.
- **"The device can"** means the browser and device can decode or encode the format. The tool checks this itself (RULE-22).
- **s** is the frame's shorter side in output pixels.
- **MB** means 1,000,000 bytes.
- **Frame times.** Frame *k* of the project starts at floor(*k* × 1,000,000 ÷ rate) µs, where rate is the project frame rate (RULE-04).
  - Times on the project timeline (clip boundaries, text start and end, audio clip start) are frame starts. Source in and out points are source times and are not snapped.
  - A time the user enters goes to the nearest frame start. A time exactly halfway goes to the earlier one.
  - A duration of *n* frames is stored as floor(*n* × 1,000,000 ÷ rate) µs. Its frame count is round(*d* × rate ÷ 1,000,000).
  - A duration the user picks in 0.1 s steps is applied as the nearest whole number of frames (exactly halfway rounds up). If that falls below the item's minimum, the minimum's whole-frame value is used.
  - A minimum duration (such as 0.1 s) rounds **up** to whole frames. No edit, speed change or frame-rate change takes a clip, fade or cross-fade below its minimum; it rounds up instead, or for speed, stops (RULE-06).
  - "Half" of a length in frames rounds down.
  - Clip boundaries on the main track are computed cumulatively from time 0, so INV-03 holds exactly.
- **Source frames.** The picture at project time *t* comes from the source frame whose display interval contains the source time that *t* maps to (through the clip's in point and speed). This holds whatever the source's frame rate, including variable rates.
- **Touch and pointer.** "On touch" applies to input from a touch contact. Mouse, pen and trackpad input on the same device uses the pointer behaviour, chosen per input, not per device.
- **Fixed times.** Text layers and audio clips keep their start and end times when main-track clips are trimmed, split, moved, deleted, sped up or added. They don't follow the footage (D-016).

| **RULE-01** | **Accepted inputs.** The tool reads the file's contents to decide its type. The file name and declared type are ignored.<br>*Video:* MP4/MOV and WebM/Matroska files whose video the device can decode. Their sound is used when the device can decode it; otherwise the clip is silent and COPY-notice.no_sound says so. High-dynamic-range video is converted to standard range, the same way in the preview and in every export. Output is always standard range (D-014).<br>*Photo:* PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (first frame only), and AVIF where the device can decode it. The orientation stored in the file is applied.<br>*Audio:* MP3, AAC/M4A, WAV, Ogg/Opus, FLAC and WebM audio where the device can decode it.<br>*Refusals.* A file is refused with a reason, leaving the project unchanged, when it is: of any other type, including HEIC photos, subtitles and project files (COPY-reason.unsupported); one the device can't decode (COPY-reason.codec); protected against copying (COPY-reason.protected); or without usable picture or sound (COPY-reason.no_media).<br>*Folders* are ignored with COPY-reason.folder.<br>*Kept as a source:* a file that passes these checks and RULE-02 is kept as a source in the media panel even when its placement on the timeline is refused (RULE-03, RULE-12); the placement notice says why. Only RULE-01 and RULE-02 refusals reject the file.<br>*Several files at once:* each is checked on its own. Accepted files are added in the order given, and refused ones are listed together in COPY-error.refused_list. |
| **RULE-02** | **Input limits.** A file is refused when:<br>• it is larger than 4,000,000,000 bytes (COPY-reason.too_large);<br>• it is a video or audio source longer than 3 hours (COPY-reason.too_long);<br>• it is video more than 5120 pixels on a side or larger than 14,745,600 pixels (5120 × 2880), or a photo more than 32,768 pixels on a side or larger than 268,435,456 pixels (COPY-reason.too_big_picture);<br>• it is a video or audio source shorter than 0.125 s, the 0.1 s minimum length (RULE-05) rounded up to whole frames at 24 fps, so no frame-rate change can take a clip of it below the minimum (COPY-reason.too_short);<br>• the project already has 100 sources (COPY-reason.too_many).<br>Video larger than 3840 × 2160 is used scaled down to fit inside 3840 × 2160, keeping its aspect ratio (D-012). A photo larger than 16,384 pixels on a side or 16,777,216 pixels in total is scaled down on load to 4096 pixels on its longer side. Bundled music tracks (RULE-12) are not sources and don't count toward the limit. |
| **RULE-03** | **Project length.** The project is at most 600 seconds (10 minutes) long (INV-04). Edits that would make it longer are handled as follows:<br>• Added media is placed up to the limit (a video trimmed at its end, a photo given a shorter duration), with COPY-notice.length_limited. Media is refused with COPY-notice.length_full when less than the minimum length (RULE-05) fits.<br>• A trim, speed or duration change stops at the limit, with COPY-notice.length_limited.<br>• A duplicate is shortened at its end to fit, or refused with COPY-notice.length_full when less than the minimum length (RULE-05) fits.<br>• A reorder, or a cross-fade being removed or shortened, is refused with COPY-notice.length_full.<br>• A frame-rate change shortens the last clip at its end to fit, with COPY-notice.length_limited.<br>Export needs at least one clip on the main track (COPY-notice.needs_clip). |
| **RULE-04** | **Frame.**<br>*Aspect:* 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9 or Original (the default). Original takes its shape from the first main-track video clip as displayed, including the user's 90° rotation (RULE-07), or from the first photo when there is no video (D-015). With the main track empty, Original is 16:9 until the first clip is added.<br>*Frame rate:* 24, 25, 30 or 60 frames per second. The default is 30. Until the user chooses a rate, the first video added at 50 fps or more sets 60.<br>*Background,* seen wherever a clip doesn't cover the frame: black (the default), white, a palette colour (RULE-11), or Blur. Blur fills the frame with a blurred, enlarged copy of the clip, showing its adjustments and look. During a fade, the blurred copy fades with the clip, to black. During a cross-fade, each clip blends together with its own blurred copy.<br>*Changes:* changing the aspect or frame rate keeps every clip, text layer and audio clip. Times move to frame starts at the new rate (RULE-03 applies). An audio clip that then overlaps the one before it moves to the next free frame start (INV-06). |
| **RULE-05** | **Clip timing.**<br>*Minimum length:* 0.1 s for every clip, text layer and audio clip, rounded up to whole frames.<br>*Photo duration:* default 3 s, settable from 0.1 s to 60 s.<br>*Adding:* video and photo sources go to the end of the main track, in the order given. Dropped onto the timeline, they go to the clip boundary nearest the drop point.<br>*Trim:* changes a video clip's in or out point within its source, or a photo's duration. Later clips move so there are no gaps (INV-03), and a video can't extend beyond its source.<br>*Split:* at the playhead, when it is at least 0.1 s from both ends of the clip. Both halves keep every setting. Only the first half keeps the fade in, and only the second half keeps the fade out and the cross-fade into the next clip. A split inside a cross-fade removes that cross-fade.<br>*Delete:* later clips move earlier to close the gap. The cross-fades into and out of the deleted clip are removed.<br>*Reorder:* moves a clip to another boundary. Afterwards, a cross-fade stays only if the same two clips are still adjacent in the same order (RULE-03 applies).<br>*Duplicate:* the copy goes right after the original and takes the original's cross-fade into the next clip; the original then has none. |
| **RULE-06** | **Speed** (video clips only). Range 0.25× to 4× in steps of 0.05×. Quick choices: 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 1.5×, 2× and 4×. The default is 1×. The clip's timeline length is its source range divided by the speed, rounded to whole frames. Sound keeps its pitch. A change that would break RULE-03 stops at the slowest step that fits. A speed increase stops at the fastest step that keeps the clip at its minimum length (RULE-05), with COPY-notice.length_limited. |
| **RULE-07** | **Placement** (video and photo clips).<br>*Mode:* Fit (default), where the whole clip is visible and the background fills the rest, or Fill, where the clip covers the frame and its edges are cut off. Both are computed on the rotated clip.<br>*Scale:* 50 % to 400 % of the mode's size, in 1 % steps; default 100 %.<br>*Position:* the clip's centre as an offset from the frame's centre, in fractions of the frame's width and height. The clip may move partly or fully outside the frame.<br>*Rotate:* in 90° clockwise steps.<br>*Flip:* horizontal and/or vertical. A flip mirrors the picture as displayed, after the rotation.<br>*Nudge:* a position control moves the clip by 1 % of the frame, or 10 % with Shift.<br>*Reset:* returns all of the above to default.<br>*Snapping:* while moving in the preview, the clip's centre snaps to the frame's centre lines within 8 screen pixels while snapping is on (RULE-14). |
| **RULE-08** | **Adjustments** (video and photo clips).<br>*Ranges:* Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Saturation, Vibrance, Temperature and Tint from −100 to +100, default 0. Sharpen, Vignette, Grain and Fade from 0 to 100, default 0.<br>*Order applied:* Temperature and Tint; Brightness, Contrast, Highlights and Shadows; Saturation and Vibrance; the look (RULE-09); Fade; Sharpen; Vignette; Grain.<br>*Quality, on a 256-step 8-bit grey ramp, with Grain at 0 and Vignette judged at each position separately:*<br>• With every adjustment at default and no look, the output matches the input within ΔE00 < 0.5.<br>• In the tool's full-precision result, before it is reduced to 8 bits per channel, at any setting: the output never reverses order, steps that don't clip stay distinct, and no two adjacent steps differ by more than twice the largest difference at default.<br>• In the 8-bit preview and export, at any setting: no step reverses by more than ΔL* 0.5, and every step is within ΔE00 1 of the full-precision result.<br>*Grain:* its size is relative to `s`. At the same output resolution and project time it is pixel-identical on every play and in the export.<br>*Reset and copy:* each adjustment can be reset alone, or all at once with Reset all. *Apply to all* copies the clip's adjustments and look to every other main-track clip as one undo step. *Copy* and *paste* move them between clips.<br>*Compare:* holding the compare control (or its key) shows the selected clip without adjustments and look. |
| **RULE-09** | **Looks.** At most one look per clip, or None (the default). The tool ships at least 16 looks of its own in four groups: Natural, Film, Cinematic, and Black & White (`30-experience/looks.md`).<br>*Intensity:* 0 to 100, default 100. At 0 the result equals None.<br>*Choosing:* choosing a look keeps the clip's adjustments. Pointer hover or keyboard focus on a look previews it on the selected clip without an undo step. A click, tap or Enter chooses it as one undo step. On touch, every tap is a choice.<br>*Thumbnails:* each look's thumbnail shows the playhead frame when the playhead is inside the selected clip, otherwise the clip's first frame. It includes the clip's adjustments, with the look at intensity 100. |
| **RULE-10** | **Fades.** Durations are picked in 0.1 s steps and applied as whole frames.<br>*Clip fade in and fade out:* 0 to 3 s each, default 0. The quick toggle sets 0.5 s. Picture opacity changes along a straight line toward the background (RULE-04), and sound gain along a straight line toward silence. Fade in plus fade out never exceeds the clip's length.<br>*Cross-fade:* between two adjacent clips, 0.1 to 2 s, default 0.5 s, never longer than half of either clip. The clips overlap by its duration. Picture blends along a straight line; sound uses an equal-power curve. Where clips are joined by a cross-fade, the earlier clip's fade out and the later clip's fade in are not applied. Between two clips where either is too short to hold a 0.1 s cross-fade within half its length, the cross-fade is unavailable, with COPY-notice.crossfade_too_short.<br>*When a clip gets shorter* (trim, speed, duration, split): its cross-fades shrink to half its length, and a cross-fade that would fall below 0.1 s is removed. Its fade in and fade out shrink in proportion to fit.<br>*Text fade:* on or off. It lasts 0.3 s in and out, or half the layer's length each when shorter.<br>*Audio clip fade in and fade out:* 0 to 5 s each, default 0, with a straight-line gain. Together they never exceed the clip's length. |
| **RULE-11** | **Text.**<br>*Limits:* at most 50 text layers. Each holds up to 500 characters, counted as user-perceived characters. Text is kept in composed form (Unicode NFC), and shortening never splits a character. Lines break where typed and wrap at 90 % of the frame's width.<br>*Typefaces:* four, all covering Vietnamese: Sans (default), Display, Serif and Rounded.<br>*Size:* 2 % to 20 % of `s`, default 6 %.<br>*Palette:* white (default), black, red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple and pink.<br>*Style:* Plain; Outline (default), 8 % of the size; Shadow; or Box, a rounded box at 70 % opacity. The outline, shadow and default box colour is black, or white when the text is black, blue or purple. The box colour can be changed to any palette colour.<br>*Alignment:* left, centre (default) or right.<br>*Position:* the text block's centre, as fractions of the frame's width and height from its top-left corner. The default is (0.5, 0.8). While moving, the block's centre snaps to the frame's centre lines, and its nearest edge snaps to a safe margin (5 % of the width at the sides, 5 % of the height at the top and bottom), within 8 screen pixels while snapping is on.<br>*Time:* a new layer starts at the playhead and lasts 3 s. If that runs past the project end, it ends at the end and starts earlier if needed to stay at least 0.1 s long. In an empty project it runs from 0 to 3 s.<br>*Fade:* a new layer's text fade (RULE-10) is off.<br>*Duplicate:* a duplicated text layer has the same times and draws on top.<br>*Empty text:* a layer whose content is empty or only whitespace when editing ends is removed. |
| **RULE-12** | **Sound.**<br>*Volume:* 0 % to 200 % per video clip and per audio clip, default 100 %. It is linear amplitude: 50 % is −6.0 dB. Mute silences without losing the value.<br>*Mute original sound* (D-022): a one-time action that mutes every main-track clip that exists now, as one undo step. When all of them are already muted, the same control offers Unmute original sound, which unmutes them all.<br>*Placing on the audio track:* a new audio clip starts at the playhead. When the main track is empty, or the playhead is at the project end, it starts at 0 instead. If that start is inside an audio clip, it moves to that clip's end. The clip ends at its source's end, cut where the next audio clip starts, and may extend past the project end (INV-07). If less than 0.1 s fits, or (with a non-empty main track) the start is at or past the project end, it is refused with COPY-notice.no_audio_room.<br>*Editing audio clips:* they can be trimmed, split, moved, deleted and duplicated like clips, and gaps may remain (INV-06). A duplicated audio clip's requested start is the original's end. A moved or duplicated audio clip that would overlap another goes to the free time where it fits whole whose start is nearest the requested start. A moved clip's own original place counts as free, and the start must be before the project end (or 0 with an empty main track). If no such time exists, or the result is the clip's original place, nothing changes and no undo step is recorded (D-021).<br>*Mix:* the sound of every unmuted video clip and audio clip is added together, then passed through a limiter that keeps the true peak at or below −1 dBTP.<br>*Bundled music:* at least 8 tracks (ENT-MusicTrack), each released into the public domain (CC0 or equivalent), with title, source and licence shown in the tool. Their total size follows NFR-10. |
| **RULE-13** | **History.**<br>*Undo steps:* every completed edit is one step, including adding or removing media (a batch of files added together is one step), every timeline edit, every clip, text or audio setting, and frame changes. One continuous drag or slider movement is one step, as is *Apply to all* or *Mute original sound*. Adding a text layer plus typing until editing ends is one step, and each later editing session is one step; removing empty text (RULE-11) is part of that step.<br>*Not undo steps:* playing, moving the playhead, timeline zoom and scroll, selection, opening panels, look previews (RULE-09), export settings and snapping.<br>*Depth:* at least the last 200 steps are kept.<br>*After undo or redo:* the item the step changed is selected if it still exists, otherwise nothing is selected. The playhead is limited to the project end.<br>Exporting doesn't clear the history. A new project (RULE-23) empties it and can't be undone. |
| **RULE-14** | **Timeline view.**<br>*Zoom:* from showing the whole project to one frame being at least 12 screen pixels wide.<br>*Snapping:* on by default, toggled for both the timeline and the preview. While on, the start or end of a dragged item, or the playhead, snaps within 8 screen pixels to: time 0, the project end, the playhead, and the starts and ends of clips, text layers and audio clips.<br>*During playback:* the view follows the playhead. With a pointer, a manual scroll stops following until playback restarts or the playhead comes back into view.<br>*On touch* (D-013): the playhead stays fixed at the timeline's horizontal centre, and scrolling the timeline moves the project under it. Scrolling therefore scrubs; a touch scroll during playback pauses it first. |
| **RULE-15** | **Video export.**<br>*Output:* MP4 with H.264 picture and AAC sound when the device can encode them. Otherwise it is the first format in the fallback order set in `40-technical` that the device can encode, named in COPY-export.format_note.<br>*Resolution* (the frame's shorter side): 480, 720, 1080, 1440 or 2160. A size is offered only when the device can encode it, and above 1080 only when a video or photo source's shorter side, after rotation and after the RULE-02 scale-down, is at least that size. The default is 1080, or the largest offered size below it.<br>*Frame rate:* the project's.<br>*Quality:* Smaller, Balanced (default) or Best, or a size target Under X MB, with quick choices 8, 10, 25 and 50 or any whole number from 1 to 4000.<br>*Meeting a size target:* the file is at most X × 1,000,000 bytes. A target can be met at a resolution only if the video bitrate stays at or above that resolution's floor (`40-technical`). If it can't be met at the chosen resolution, the panel says so and offers the largest resolution that can meet it. If no resolution can, export is unavailable and the panel offers, as one choice, the smallest whole-MB target that 480 can meet (D-020).<br>*Sound:* included when the project has any unmuted, non-silent sound.<br>*Contents:* no location, device, source names or other metadata beyond what the format requires.<br>*File name:* `clip-<YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS>.<ext>` in local time, when the export finished.<br>*Success:* the file has been handed to the browser to save, and the browser hasn't reported the save as cancelled or failed (D-018). |
| **RULE-16** | **Other exports.**<br>*GIF:* a range of at most 15 s. When the project is 15 s or less, the range is the whole project. Otherwise the user picks it, defaulting to the 15 s from the playhead, or the last 15 s when fewer than 15 s remain. Size: 320, 480 (default) or 640 pixels on the longer side. Frame rate: 10 or 15 (default). It loops forever and has no sound.<br>*Audio only:* the whole project's mix (RULE-12), available when the project has a clip and any unmuted, non-silent sound. Formats: a compressed one (AAC in M4A when the device can encode it, otherwise the fallback in `40-technical`), or WAV (16-bit, 48 kHz, stereo), which is always available.<br>*Success and file names* follow RULE-15, with the right extension. |
| **RULE-17** | **Host contract.**<br>*From the host:* the initial theme (`light` or `dark`) and language (RULE-18) come in the tool's address. Later changes arrive as settings messages, accepted only from an allowed origin and only from the page that embeds the tool.<br>*To the host:* the tool sends one message only, a ready signal without content.<br>*Allowed origins* are fixed at deployment. They decide who may embed the tool and whose messages count. An empty list means standalone only.<br>*Frame grants:* the host frame allows downloads and leave-page dialogs, and moves keyboard focus into the tool after the ready signal. Opening new tabs, full screen and web share are optional (without full screen, the preview maximises within the page; without web share, Share is hidden and only download is offered). |
| **RULE-18** | **Language.** Supported: `en-US` (the default) and `vi-VN`. A value matches when it is equal ignoring case, or else when its primary language (`en`, `vi`) matches.<br>*Active language,* in order: the latest supported value from an accepted host message; else the supported value in the address; else the first matching browser-preferred language; else `en-US`.<br>*Effect:* it sets every user-facing string, the page language, and number, duration and plural forms. It never changes exports (INV-05) and is never stored. |
| **RULE-19** | **Unexported changes.** The project has unexported changes when it has at least one clip, text layer or audio clip, and an undo step (RULE-13) has been made, undone or redone since the session began or since the last successful full-project export. Only a video export (RULE-15) or an audio export (RULE-16) counts as full-project; a GIF export never clears unexported changes (D-019). |
| **RULE-20** | **Keyboard map** (with a physical keyboard).<br>*Scope:* keys act while focus is in the tool and no text is being edited. While a text or number field is being edited, only Esc and Mod+Enter (finish editing) keep their meaning. While a dialog or menu is open, only Esc acts. A focused control that uses a key itself gets it: sliders get the arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down; buttons and toggles get Space and Enter.<br>*Playback:* Space plays and pauses. Left/Right move one frame (Shift: 1 s). Home/End go to the start/end.<br>*Edit:*<br>• S or Mod+B: split at the playhead (the selected clip, else the main-track clip under the playhead).<br>• I / O: start the selected item here / end it here.<br>• Delete or Backspace: delete the selection.<br>• Mod+D: duplicate.<br>• Alt+Left / Alt+Right: move the selected clip one place earlier/later, or a text layer or audio clip one frame (Shift: 1 s). Nudging an audio clip follows the RULE-12 move rule.<br>• M: mute or unmute the selected clip.<br>• T: add a text layer.<br>• Mod+C / Mod+V: copy and paste a clip's adjustments and look; they do nothing unless a video or photo clip is selected. Mod+V adds media when the clipboard holds files.<br>• Hold \\ : compare (RULE-08).<br>*History:* Mod+Z undo; Mod+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y redo.<br>*View:* = and - zoom the timeline; Shift+Z fits it; N toggles snapping.<br>*Files:* Mod+O adds media; Mod+E opens export.<br>*Help:* ? shows this list.<br>Letter keys follow the key's position on a US layout. The tool takes these keys from the browser only within this scope. |
| **RULE-21** | **Touch.**<br>*Selecting:* a tap selects, and a tap on empty space clears the selection.<br>*Timeline:* a one-finger horizontal drag scrolls it, which scrubs (RULE-14). A two-finger pinch zooms it. Dragging a trim handle trims. Pressing and holding for 0.4 s, then dragging, moves a clip, text layer or audio clip.<br>*Preview:* dragging the selected clip or text moves it, and pinching scales it.<br>*Equivalent controls:* every gesture has a visible control (NFR-06):<br>• pinch zoom → zoom controls;<br>• hold-and-drag → move-earlier/later controls (clips) and time controls (text layers, audio clips);<br>• trim drag → start-here and end-here controls;<br>• preview drag and pinch → placement and text position controls;<br>• scrub → frame-step and play controls.<br>*Touch targets:* at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels. |
| **RULE-22** | **Device support.** On opening, the tool checks that the device can decode video, can encode at least one video output format (RULE-15), and can draw effects at all. The encode check needs a video encoder only: a device that can encode no sound format still counts as supported, and only the exports that need a sound encoder are left out (WAV audio stays available, RULE-16). If any check fails, the tool explains each failure (COPY-unsupported.missing_decode, COPY-unsupported.missing_encode, COPY-unsupported.missing_effects) and accepts no media. If effects draw below the speed threshold set in `40-technical`, the editor opens anyway, with COPY-notice.slow_device. When the device decodes a source slower than real time, the source is still accepted and COPY-notice.slow_device is shown once for the session; the preview then shows the newest frame it can while sound plays on, and exports are not affected. When a single source can't be decoded, or a single export format can't be encoded, only that item is refused or left out (PRN-07). |
| **RULE-23** | **New project.** Starting a new project removes every source, clip, text layer and audio clip, empties the history (RULE-13), and releases their memory. Frame settings return to RULE-04 defaults, including frame-rate detection, and copied adjustments are cleared. Export settings, snapping and timeline zoom are kept. |
