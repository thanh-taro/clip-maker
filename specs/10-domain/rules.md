# Rules

## Conventions
- **Mod** means Ctrl on Windows/Linux and Cmd on macOS.
- **"The device can"** means the browser and device can decode or encode the format. The tool checks this itself (RULE-22).
- **s** is the frame's shorter side in output pixels.
- **MB** means 1,000,000 bytes.
- **Frame times.** Frame *k* of the project starts at floor(*k* × 1,000,000 ÷ rate) µs, where rate is the project frame rate (RULE-04).
  - Times on the project timeline (clip boundaries, text start and end, audio clip start) are frame starts. Source in and out points are source times and are not snapped.
  - A time the user enters goes to the nearest frame start. A time exactly halfway goes to the earlier one.
  - A duration of *n* frames is stored as floor(*n* × 1,000,000 ÷ rate) µs. Its frame count is round(*d* × rate ÷ 1,000,000).
  - A duration the user picks in 0.1 s steps is applied as the nearest whole number of frames (exactly halfway rounds up). If that falls below the item's minimum, the minimum's whole-frame value is used.
  - A minimum duration (such as 0.1 s) rounds **up** to whole frames. No edit, speed change or frame-rate change takes a clip, fade or cross-fade below its minimum; it rounds up instead, or for speed, stops (RULE-06).
  - "Half" of a length in frames rounds down.
  - Clip boundaries on the main track are computed cumulatively from time 0, so INV-03 holds exactly.
- **Source frames.** The picture at project time *t* comes from the source frame whose display interval contains the source time that *t* maps to (through the clip's in point and speed). This holds whatever the source's frame rate, including variable rates.
- **Touch and pointer.** "On touch" applies to input from a touch contact. Mouse, pen and trackpad input on the same device uses the pointer behaviour, chosen per input, not per device.
- **Fixed times.** Text layers and audio clips keep their start and end times when main-track clips are trimmed, split, moved, deleted, sped up or added. They don't follow the footage (D-016).

| **RULE-01** | **Accepted inputs.** The tool reads the file's contents to decide its type. The file name and declared type are ignored.<br>*Video:* MP4/MOV and WebM/Matroska files whose video the device can decode. Their sound is used when the device can decode it; otherwise the clip is silent and COPY-notice.no_sound says so. High-dynamic-range video is converted to standard range, the same way in the preview and in every export. Output is always standard range (D-014).<br>*Photo:* PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (first frame only), and AVIF where the device can decode it. The orientation stored in the file is applied.<br>*Audio:* MP3, AAC/M4A, WAV, Ogg/Opus, FLAC and WebM audio where the device can decode it.<br>*Refusals.* A file is refused with a reason, leaving the project unchanged, when it is: of any other type, including HEIC photos, subtitles and project files (COPY-reason.unsupported); one the device can't decode (COPY-reason.codec); protected against copying (COPY-reason.protected); or without usable picture or sound (COPY-reason.no_media).<br>*Folders* are ignored with COPY-reason.folder.<br>*Kept as a source:* a file that passes these checks and RULE-02 is kept as a source in the media panel even when its placement on the timeline is refused (RULE-03, RULE-12); the placement notice says why. Only RULE-01 and RULE-02 refusals reject the file.<br>*Several files at once:* each is checked on its own. Accepted files are added in the order given, and refused ones are listed together in COPY-error.refused_list. |
| **RULE-02** | **Input limits.** A file is refused when:<br>• it is larger than 4,000,000,000 bytes (COPY-reason.too_large);<br>• it is a video or audio source longer than 3 hours (COPY-reason.too_long);<br>• it is video more than 5120 pixels on a side or larger than 14,745,600 pixels (5120 × 2880), or a photo more than 32,768 pixels on a side or larger than 268,435,456 pixels (COPY-reason.too_big_picture);<br>• it is a video or audio source shorter than 0.125 s, the 0.1 s minimum length (RULE-05) rounded up to whole frames at 24 fps, so no frame-rate change can take a clip of it below the minimum (COPY-reason.too_short);<br>• the project already has 100 sources (COPY-reason.too_many).<br>Video larger than 3840 × 2160 is used scaled down to fit inside 3840 × 2160, keeping its aspect ratio (D-012). A photo larger than 16,384 pixels on a side or 16,777,216 pixels in total is scaled down on load to 4096 pixels on its longer side. |
| **RULE-03** | **Project length.** The project is at most 600 seconds (10 minutes) long (INV-04). Edits that would make it longer are handled as follows:<br>• Added media is placed up to the limit (a video trimmed at its end, a photo given a shorter duration), with COPY-notice.length_limited. Media is refused with COPY-notice.length_full when less than the minimum length (RULE-05) fits.<br>• A trim, speed or duration change stops at the limit, with COPY-notice.length_limited.<br>• A duplicate is shortened at its end to fit, or refused with COPY-notice.length_full when less than the minimum length (RULE-05) fits.<br>• A reorder, or a cross-fade being removed or shortened, is refused with COPY-notice.length_full.<br>• A frame-rate change shortens the last clip at its end to fit, with COPY-notice.length_limited. It is refused with COPY-notice.length_full, and nothing changes, when the last clip can't absorb the excess even at its minimum length, or when a clip can't reach the new rate's minimum length (RULE-05) because its source's end is unknown.<br>Export needs at least one clip on the main track (COPY-notice.needs_clip). |
| **RULE-04** | **Frame.**<br>*Aspect:* 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9 or Original (the default). Original takes its shape from the first main-track video clip as displayed, including the user's 90° rotation (RULE-07), or from the first photo when there is no video (D-015). With the main track empty, Original is 16:9 until the first clip is added.<br>*Frame rate:* 24, 25, 30 or 60 frames per second. The default is 30. Until the user chooses a rate, the first video added at 50 fps or more sets 60.<br>*Background,* seen wherever a clip doesn't cover the frame: black (the default), white, a palette colour (RULE-11), or Blur. Blur fills the frame with a blurred, enlarged copy of the clip, showing its adjustments and look. During a fade, the blurred copy fades with the clip, to black. During a cross-fade, each clip blends together with its own blurred copy.<br>*Changes:* changing the aspect or frame rate keeps every clip, text layer and audio clip. Times move to frame starts at the new rate (RULE-03 applies). An audio clip that then overlaps the one before it moves to the next free frame start (INV-06). |
| **RULE-05** | **Clip timing.**<br>*Minimum length:* 0.1 s for every clip, text layer and audio clip, rounded up to whole frames.<br>*Photo duration:* default 3 s, settable from 0.1 s to 60 s in 0.1 s steps, applied as whole frames.<br>*Adding:* video and photo sources go to the end of the main track, in the order given. Dropped onto the timeline, they go to the clip boundary nearest the drop point.<br>*Trim:* changes a video clip's in or out point within its source, or a photo's duration. Later clips move so there are no gaps (INV-03), and a video can't extend beyond its source.<br>*Split:* at the playhead, when it is at least 0.1 s from both ends of the clip. Both halves keep every setting. Only the first half keeps the fade in, and only the second half keeps the fade out and the cross-fade into the next clip. A split inside a cross-fade removes that cross-fade.<br>*Delete:* later clips move earlier to close the gap. The cross-fades into and out of the deleted clip are removed.<br>*Reorder:* moves a clip to another boundary. Afterwards, a cross-fade stays only if the same two clips are still adjacent in the same order (RULE-03 applies).<br>*Duplicate:* the copy goes right after the original and takes the original's cross-fade into the next clip; the original then has none. |
| **RULE-06** | **Speed** (video clips only). Range 0.25× to 4× in steps of 0.05×. Quick choices: 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 1.5×, 2× and 4×. The default is 1×. The clip's timeline length is its source range divided by the speed, rounded to whole frames. Sound keeps its pitch. A change that would break RULE-03 stops at the slowest step that fits. A speed increase stops at the fastest step that keeps the clip at its minimum length (RULE-05), with COPY-notice.speed_limited. |
| **RULE-07** | **Placement** (video and photo clips).<br>*Mode:* Fit (default), where the whole clip is visible and the background fills the rest, or Fill, where the clip covers the frame and its edges are cut off. Both are computed on the rotated clip.<br>*Scale:* 50 % to 400 % of the mode's size, in 1 % steps; default 100 %.<br>*Position:* the clip's centre as an offset from the frame's centre, in fractions of the frame's width and height. The clip may move partly or fully outside the frame, its centre at most 10 frame widths or heights from the frame's centre.<br>*Rotate:* in 90° clockwise steps.<br>*Flip:* horizontal and/or vertical. A flip mirrors the picture as displayed, after the rotation.<br>*Nudge:* a position control moves the clip by 1 % of the frame, or 10 % with Shift.<br>*Reset:* returns all of the above to default.<br>*Snapping:* while moving in the preview, the clip's centre snaps to the frame's centre lines within 8 screen pixels while snapping is on (RULE-14). |
| **RULE-08** | **Adjustments** (video and photo clips).<br>*Ranges:* Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Saturation, Vibrance, Temperature and Tint from −100 to +100, default 0. Sharpen, Vignette, Grain and Fade from 0 to 100, default 0.<br>*Order applied:* Temperature and Tint; Brightness, Contrast, Highlights and Shadows; Saturation and Vibrance; the look (RULE-09); Fade; Sharpen; Vignette; Grain.<br>*Quality, on a 256-step 8-bit grey ramp, with Grain at 0 and Vignette judged at each position separately:*<br>• With every adjustment at default and no look, the output matches the input within ΔE00 < 0.5.<br>• In the tool's full-precision result, before it is reduced to 8 bits per channel, at any setting: the output never reverses order, steps that don't clip stay distinct, and no two adjacent steps differ by more than twice the largest difference at default.<br>• In the 8-bit preview and the frame the export encodes, at any setting: no step reverses by more than ΔL* 0.5, and every step is within ΔE00 1 of the full-precision result. The exported file matches the preview within NFR-08.<br>*Grain:* its size is relative to `s`. At the same output resolution and project time it is pixel-identical on every play and in the export.<br>*Reset and copy:* each adjustment can be reset alone, or all at once with Reset all. *Apply to all* copies the clip's adjustments and look to every other main-track clip as one undo step. *Copy* and *paste* move them between clips.<br>*Compare:* holding the compare control (or its key) shows the selected clip without adjustments and look.<br>*Drag detent:* a pointer drag of a slider for an adjustment from −100 to +100 that lands within ±2 of the default lands on the default (0). Keys and the number field still set ±1 and ±2.<br>*Colour tools* (D-053): RULE-24 places them in the order applied, and adds them to Reset all, Apply to all, Copy, Paste and Compare. |
| **RULE-09** | **Looks.** At most one look per clip, or None (the default). The tool ships at least 16 looks of its own in four groups: Natural, Film, Cinematic, and Black & White (`30-experience/looks.md`).<br>*Intensity:* 0 to 100, default 100; choosing another look starts it at 100. At 0 the result equals None.<br>*Choosing:* choosing a look keeps the clip's adjustments. Pointer hover or keyboard focus on a look previews it on the selected clip without an undo step. A click, tap or Enter chooses it as one undo step. On touch, every tap is a choice.<br>*Thumbnails:* each look's thumbnail shows the playhead frame when the playhead is inside the selected clip, otherwise the clip's first frame. It includes the clip's adjustments and colour tools (RULE-24), with the look at intensity 100. |
| **RULE-10** | **Fades.** Durations are picked in 0.1 s steps and applied as whole frames.<br>*Clip fade in and fade out:* 0 to 3 s each, default 0. The quick toggle sets 0.5 s. Picture opacity changes along a straight line toward the background (RULE-04), and sound gain along a straight line toward silence. Fade in plus fade out never exceeds the clip's length.<br>*Cross-fade:* between two adjacent clips, 0.1 to 2 s, default 0.5 s, never longer than half of either clip. The clips overlap by its duration. Picture blends along a straight line; sound uses an equal-power curve. Where clips are joined by a cross-fade, the earlier clip's fade out and the later clip's fade in are not applied. Between two clips where either is too short to hold a 0.1 s cross-fade within half its length, the cross-fade is unavailable, with COPY-notice.crossfade_too_short.<br>*When a clip gets shorter* (trim, speed, duration, split): its cross-fades shrink to half its length, and a cross-fade that would fall below 0.1 s is removed. Its fade in and fade out shrink in proportion to fit.<br>*Text fade:* on or off. It lasts 0.3 s in and out, or half the layer's length each when shorter.<br>*Audio clip fade in and fade out:* 0 to 5 s each, default 0, with a straight-line gain. Together they never exceed the clip's length. |
| **RULE-11** | **Text.**<br>*Limits:* at most 50 text layers. Each holds up to 500 characters, counted as user-perceived characters. Text is kept in composed form (Unicode NFC), and shortening never splits a character. Lines break where typed and wrap at 90 % of the frame's width.<br>*Typefaces:* four, all covering Vietnamese: Sans (default), Display, Serif and Rounded.<br>*Size:* 2 % to 20 % of `s` in whole percent, default 6 %.<br>*Palette:* white (default), black, red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple and pink.<br>*Style:* Plain; Outline (default), 8 % of the size; Shadow; or Box, a rounded box at 70 % opacity. The outline, shadow and default box colour is black, or white when the text is black, blue or purple. The box colour can be changed to any palette colour.<br>*Alignment:* left, centre (default) or right.<br>*Position:* the text block's centre, as fractions of the frame's width and height from its top-left corner. The default is (0.5, 0.8). While moving, the block's centre snaps to the frame's centre lines, and its nearest edge snaps to a safe margin (5 % of the width at the sides, 5 % of the height at the top and bottom), within 8 screen pixels while snapping is on. A position control nudges the block's centre by 1 % of the frame, or 10 % with Shift, keeping it within the frame.<br>*Time:* a new layer starts at the playhead and lasts 3 s. If that runs past the project end, it ends at the end and starts earlier if needed to stay at least 0.1 s long. In an empty project it runs from 0 to 3 s.<br>*Fade:* a new layer's text fade (RULE-10) is off.<br>*Duplicate:* a duplicated text layer has the same times and draws on top.<br>*Empty text:* a layer whose content is empty or only whitespace when editing ends is removed. |
| **RULE-12** | **Sound.**<br>*Volume:* 0 % to 200 % per video clip and per audio clip, default 100 %. It is linear amplitude: 50 % is −6.0 dB. Mute silences without losing the value.<br>*Mute original sound* (D-022): a one-time action that mutes every main-track clip that exists now, as one undo step. When all of them are already muted, the same control offers Unmute original sound, which unmutes them all.<br>*Placing on the audio track:* a new audio clip starts at the playhead. When the main track is empty, or the playhead is at the project end, it starts at 0 instead. If that start is inside an audio clip, it moves to that clip's end. The clip ends at its source's end, cut where the next audio clip starts, and may extend past the project end (INV-07). If less than 0.1 s fits, or (with a non-empty main track) the start is at or past the project end, it is refused with COPY-notice.no_audio_room.<br>*Editing audio clips:* they can be trimmed, split, moved, deleted and duplicated like clips, and gaps may remain (INV-06). A duplicated audio clip's requested start is the original's end. A moved or duplicated audio clip that would overlap another goes to the free time where it fits whole whose start is nearest the requested start. A moved clip's own original place counts as free, and the start must be before the project end (or 0 with an empty main track). If no such time exists, or the result is the clip's original place, nothing changes and no undo step is recorded (D-021).<br>*Mix:* the sound of every unmuted video clip and audio clip is added together, then passed through a limiter that keeps the true peak at or below −1 dBTP. |
| **RULE-13** | **History.**<br>*Undo steps:* every completed edit is one step, including adding or removing media (a batch of files added together is one step), every timeline edit, every clip, text or audio setting, and frame changes. One continuous drag or slider movement is one step, as is *Apply to all* or *Mute original sound*. Adding a text layer plus typing until editing ends is one step, and each later editing session is one step; removing empty text (RULE-11) is part of that step.<br>*Not undo steps:* playing, moving the playhead, timeline zoom and scroll, selection, opening panels, look previews (RULE-09), export settings and snapping.<br>*Depth:* at least the last 200 steps are kept.<br>*After undo or redo:* the item the step changed is selected if it is the only changed item that still exists; otherwise nothing is selected. The playhead is limited to the project end.<br>Exporting doesn't clear the history. A new project (RULE-23) empties it and can't be undone. |
| **RULE-14** | **Timeline view.**<br>*Zoom:* from showing the whole project to one frame being at least 12 screen pixels wide.<br>*Snapping:* on by default, toggled for both the timeline and the preview. While on, the start or end of a dragged item, or the playhead, snaps within 8 screen pixels to: time 0, the project end, the playhead, and the starts and ends of clips, text layers and audio clips.<br>*During playback:* the view follows the playhead. With a pointer, a manual scroll stops following until playback restarts or the playhead comes back into view.<br>*On narrow layouts* (phones, D-013): the playhead stays fixed at the timeline's horizontal centre, and scrolling the timeline, by any input, moves the project under it. Scrolling therefore scrubs; a scroll during playback pauses it first. On wider touch screens a one-finger drag scrolls the view without moving the playhead. |
| **RULE-15** | **Video export.**<br>*Output:* MP4 with H.264 picture and AAC sound when the device can encode them. Otherwise it is the first format in the fallback order set in `40-technical` that the device can encode, named in COPY-export.format_note. Format names there are technical and the same in every language: the container alone when it changes (WebM instead of MP4, Ogg instead of M4A), or the container with its codecs when only the sound codec changes ("MP4 (H.264 + Opus)" instead of "MP4 (H.264 + AAC)"). WAV gets no note.<br>*Resolution* (the frame's shorter side): 480, 720, 1080, 1440 or 2160. A size is offered only when the device can encode it, and above 1080 only when a video or photo source's shorter side, after rotation and after the RULE-02 scale-down, is at least that size. The default is 1080, or the largest offered size below it.<br>*Frame rate:* the project's.<br>*Quality:* Smaller, Balanced (default) or Best, or a size target Under X MB, with quick choices 8, 10, 25 and 50 or any whole number from 1 to 4000.<br>*Meeting a size target:* the file is at most X × 1,000,000 bytes. A target can be met at a resolution only if the video bitrate stays at or above that resolution's floor (`40-technical`). If it can't be met at the chosen resolution, the panel says so and offers the largest resolution that can meet it. If no resolution can, export is unavailable and the panel offers, as one choice, the smallest whole-MB target that 480 can meet (D-020).<br>*Sound:* included when the project has any unmuted, non-silent sound.<br>*Contents:* no location, device, source names or other metadata beyond what the format requires.<br>*File name:* `clip-<YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS>.<ext>` in local time, when the export finished.<br>*Success:* the file has been handed to the browser to save, and the browser hasn't reported the save as cancelled or failed (D-018). |
| **RULE-16** | **Other exports.**<br>*GIF:* a range of at most 15 s. When the project is 15 s or less, the range is the whole project. Otherwise the user picks it, defaulting to the 15 s from the playhead, or the last 15 s when fewer than 15 s remain. Size: 320, 480 (default) or 640 pixels on the longer side. Frame rate: 10 or 15 (default). It loops forever and has no sound.<br>*Audio only:* the whole project's mix (RULE-12), available when the project has a clip and any unmuted, non-silent sound. Formats: a compressed one (AAC in M4A when the device can encode it, otherwise the fallback in `40-technical`), or WAV (16-bit, 48 kHz, stereo), which is always available.<br>*Success and file names* follow RULE-15, with the right extension. |
| **RULE-17** | **Host contract.**<br>*From the host:* the initial theme (`light` or `dark`) and language (RULE-18) come in the tool's address. Later changes arrive as settings messages, accepted only from an allowed origin and only from the page that embeds the tool.<br>*To the host:* the tool sends one message only, a ready signal without content.<br>*Allowed origins* are fixed at deployment. They decide who may embed the tool and whose messages count. An empty list means standalone only.<br>*Frame grants:* the host frame allows downloads and leave-page dialogs, and moves keyboard focus into the tool after the ready signal. Opening new tabs, full screen and web share are optional (without full screen, the preview maximises within the page; without web share, Share is hidden and only download is offered). |
| **RULE-18** | **Language.** Supported: `en-US` (the default) and `vi-VN`. A value matches when it is equal ignoring case, or else when its primary language (`en`, `vi`) matches.<br>*Active language,* in order: the latest supported value from an accepted host message; else the supported value in the address; else the first matching browser-preferred language; else `en-US`.<br>*Effect:* it sets every user-facing string, the page language, and number, duration and plural forms. It never changes exports (INV-05) and is never stored. |
| **RULE-19** | **Unexported changes.** The project has unexported changes when it has at least one clip, text layer or audio clip, and an undo step (RULE-13) has been made, undone or redone since the session began or since the last successful full-project export. Only a video export (RULE-15) or an audio export (RULE-16) counts as full-project; a GIF export never clears unexported changes (D-019). |
| **RULE-20** | **Keyboard map** (with a physical keyboard).<br>*Scope:* keys act while focus is in the tool and no text is being edited. Single-character keys (the letter keys, with or without Shift, and =, +, -, ? and \\) act only while focus is on the preview, the timeline (its items, ruler and toolbars) or the transport controls; elsewhere, and while no control has focus, they do nothing and keep their usual meaning (WCAG 2.1.4). Space, the arrow keys, Home, End, Delete, Backspace, Esc and the combinations with Mod or Alt keep the scope of this paragraph. While a text or number field is being edited, only Esc and Mod+Enter (finish editing) keep their meaning. While a dialog or menu is open, only Esc acts. A focused control that uses a key itself gets it: sliders (including the timeline's time ruler, F-002.R8) and the compact inspector's resize grip get the arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down; the eyedropper marker (RULE-25) gets the arrow keys, Enter, Space and Esc; option groups (radio groups) get the arrow keys; tab lists (the inspector's clip tabs, the phone tab bar) get Left, Right, Home and End; a toolbar's buttons get Left and Right, which move between them; buttons and toggles get Space and Enter, except timeline items (clips, text layers, audio clips), which get Enter only, so Space plays and pauses there.<br>*Playback:* Space plays and pauses. Left/Right move one frame (Shift: 1 s). Home/End go to the start/end.<br>*Edit:*<br>• S or Mod+B: split at the playhead (the selected clip, else the main-track clip under the playhead).<br>• I / O: start the selected item here / end it here.<br>• Delete or Backspace: delete the selection.<br>• Mod+D: duplicate.<br>• Alt+Left / Alt+Right: move the selected clip one place earlier/later, or a text layer or audio clip one frame (Shift: 1 s; for a clip Shift makes no difference). Nudging an audio clip follows the RULE-12 move rule.<br>• M: mute or unmute the selected clip.<br>• T: add a text layer.<br>• Mod+C / Mod+V: copy and paste a clip's adjustments and look (and colour tools, RULE-24); they do nothing unless a video or photo clip is selected, and Mod+C copies selected page text instead when there is some. Mod+V adds media when the clipboard holds files.<br>• Hold \\ : compare (RULE-08).<br>*History:* Mod+Z undo; Mod+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y (all platforms) redo.<br>*View:* = and - zoom the timeline (+ zooms in too); Shift+Z fits it; N toggles snapping.<br>*Files:* Mod+O adds media; Mod+E opens export.<br>*Help:* ? shows this list.<br>Letter keys follow the key's position on a US layout; =, - and ? follow the character the layout types. The tool takes these keys from the browser only within this scope. |
| **RULE-21** | **Touch.**<br>*Selecting:* a tap selects, and a tap on empty space clears the selection.<br>*Timeline:* a one-finger horizontal drag scrolls it, which scrubs on narrow layouts (RULE-14). A two-finger pinch zooms it. Dragging a trim handle trims. Pressing and holding for 0.4 s, then dragging, moves a clip, text layer or audio clip.<br>*Preview:* dragging the selected clip or text moves it, and pinching scales it.<br>*Equivalent controls:* every gesture has a visible control (NFR-06):<br>• pinch zoom → zoom controls;<br>• hold-and-drag → move-earlier/later controls (clips) and time controls (text layers, audio clips);<br>• trim drag → start-here and end-here controls;<br>• preview drag and pinch → placement and text position controls;<br>• scrub → frame-step and play controls.<br>*Touch targets:* at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels. |
| **RULE-22** | **Device support.** On opening, the tool checks that the device can decode video, can encode at least one video output format (RULE-15), and can draw effects at all. The encode check needs a video encoder only: a device that can encode no sound format still counts as supported, and only the exports that need a sound encoder are left out (WAV audio stays available, RULE-16). If any check fails, the tool explains each failure (COPY-unsupported.missing_decode, COPY-unsupported.missing_encode, COPY-unsupported.missing_effects) and accepts no media. If effects draw below the speed threshold set in `40-technical`, the editor opens anyway, with COPY-notice.slow_device. When the device decodes a source slower than real time, the source is still accepted and COPY-notice.slow_device is shown once for the session; the preview then shows the newest frame it can while sound plays on, and exports are not affected. When a single source can't be decoded, or a single export format can't be encoded, only that item is refused or left out (PRN-07). |
| **RULE-23** | **New project.** Starting a new project removes every source, clip, text layer and audio clip, empties the history (RULE-13), and releases their memory. Frame settings return to RULE-04 defaults, including frame-rate detection, and copied adjustments, colour tools and look are cleared. Export settings, snapping and timeline zoom are kept. |
| **RULE-24** | **Colour tools** (video and photo clips, D-053). Five per clip: Colour mixer, Colour pop, Split toning and Duotone (this rule), and Colour replace (RULE-25). Every setting at its default leaves the picture unchanged.<br>*Measures:* the tools compute lightness, chroma and hue in the working perceptual colour space fixed in `40-technical`, with lightness on a scale from 0 (black) to 100 (white). *Lightness kept*, *hue reached* and range centres are judged in that space: lightness within 0.5 on its scale, hue within 1°. ΔE00, CIELAB C* and the grey test are judged in CIELAB. A pixel is *grey* when its C* is below 2.<br>*Hue wheel:* names a hue in degrees by the sRGB colour of that hue at full saturation and half lightness (0° red, 120° green, 240° blue); hue sliders and the colour picker use it. A tint or target of hue *x* on the hue wheel means the working-space hue of the hue wheel's colour at *x*. On the hue wheel the range centres are Red 0°, Orange 30°, Yellow 60°, Green 120°, Aqua 180°, Blue 240°, Purple 270° and Magenta 300°; a hue slider's value names the nearest one, and a hue exactly halfway between two names the later one (210° reads Blue).<br>*Hue ring* (test fixture): 360 colours, one per degree of working-space hue, all at CIELAB L* 60 and C* 30 (inside sRGB at every hue). A range's *centre step* is the step nearest that range's working-space centre hue.<br>*Colour ranges:* eight, in hue order, each centred on the working-space hue of an sRGB reference colour: Red #FF0000, Orange #FF8000, Yellow #FFFF00, Green #00FF00, Aqua #00FFFF, Blue #0000FF, Purple #8000FF, Magenta #FF00FF. After Magenta comes Red again. A pixel belongs to the two ranges whose centres surround its hue, with weights that change smoothly with hue and always add up to 1, so there are no hard edges. A range's effect fades smoothly to none as the pixel's C* falls from 12 to 2.<br>*Colour mixer:* per range, Hue, Saturation and Lightness from −100 to +100, default 0 (24 values). Hue +100 moves the range's colours to the next range's centre hue, −100 to the previous range's, in proportion between. Saturation −100 makes them grey and +100 doubles their chroma, limited to what the output can show while keeping hue and lightness. Lightness +100 moves their lightness halfway to white, −100 halfway to black. Each value acts on a pixel by its weight in that range.<br>*Colour pop:* Colour is None (the default) or one of the eight ranges; Range 0 to 100, default 50; Amount 0 to 100, default 100. With a colour chosen, pixels near that range's centre hue keep their colour and every other pixel loses Amount % of its chroma (at 100 it is grey). Range sets how far colours are kept: from 25 % (at 0) to 100 % (at 100) of the way to each neighbouring range's centre. Beyond that, keeping fades out over a further 25 % of the way, so there is never a hard edge. Choosing None turns it off and keeps Range and Amount.<br>*Split toning:* Shadows hue default 210°, Highlights hue default 35°, each 0° to 359° on the hue wheel; Shadows strength and Highlights strength 0 to 100, default 0; Balance −100 to +100, default 0. Dark tones take the shadows tint and light tones the highlights tint, each by its strength (at 100 the tint adds C* of about 20 where its weight is full), with lightness kept. The two tints cross over at lightness 50 at Balance 0, moving to 80 at −100 (more of the picture takes the shadows tint) and to 20 at +100, in proportion between. Each tint's weight is full from 15 inside its side of the crossover and fades smoothly to none 15 past it; both fade smoothly to none within 20 of black (0) and white (100).<br>*Duotone:* None (the default), one of the six built-in pairs (`30-experience/looks.md` "Duotone pairs") or Custom; Strength 0 to 100, default 100. A pair is a shadows colour and a highlights colour. Each pixel's position on a smooth gradient from the shadows colour (at black) to the highlights colour (at white) is its CIELAB L* ÷ 100 (0 at black, 1 at white), so the gradient is crossed in even steps of perceived lightness; the gradient itself is blended in the working perceptual space; Strength mixes that with the incoming picture, and at 0 the result equals None. Choosing a pair or Custom starts Strength at 100. For a built-in pair the colours are that pair's (`looks.md`). Choosing None keeps the last pair or Custom colours on the clip; Custom starts from the colours shown last on this clip (Midnight & Peach's when the clip never had a duotone), and its two colours are set with the colour picker. A duotone Reset, or Reset all, returns it to None and forgets the remembered colours, so Custom then starts from Midnight & Peach. Swap exchanges the two colours. Colours are stored as sRGB #RRGGBB.<br>*Order applied* (the whole chain for a clip, extending RULE-08):<br>1. Colour replace (RULE-25): it matches the source's own colours, so later changes to white balance or tone never break a match, and the eyedropper samples exactly what it compares.<br>2. Temperature and Tint; 3. Brightness, Contrast, Highlights and Shadows; 4. Saturation and Vibrance (RULE-08): corrections come first so ranges are judged on corrected colours.<br>5. Colour mixer: on corrected colours, before the look, so the look styles the mixed colours as it would a camera's own.<br>6. Colour pop: after the mixer, so the kept colour is the one the user sees after mixing; before the look, so the look styles the whole picture alike.<br>7. The look (RULE-09).<br>8. Split toning: after the look, as a finishing tint that the look can't undo.<br>9. Duotone: last among colour steps, so the picture always shows exactly the two chosen colours.<br>10. Fade, Sharpen, Vignette and Grain (RULE-08).<br>*Quality* (24-patch chart, the 256-step grey ramp and the hue ring), with Grain 0:<br>• With every colour tool at default, the output matches the RULE-08 result within ΔE00 < 0.5.<br>• Colour mixer, Colour pop and Colour replace never change a grey pixel by ΔE00 0.5 or more, at any setting.<br>• No hard edges: at any setting, in the full-precision result, every weight a tool uses (a range's weight, colour pop's keep weight, a replacement's match weight, split toning's tone weights, the grey fade) changes continuously with hue, chroma or lightness, and across a fade it changes by at most 1.5 ÷ (the fade's width) per hue-ring step for fades over hue (width in hue-ring steps), per lightness unit for fades over lightness (width in lightness units), per C* unit for the grey fade (C* 2 → 12), or per working-space chroma unit for colour replace's chroma fade (width a quarter of From's chroma). Where two or three fades overlap (for example split toning's crossover near the black or white fade, or a replacement's hue, chroma and grey fades), each is judged against its own bound and their product may change per step by at most the sum of their bounds.<br>• No banding: at any setting, in the full-precision result, no two adjacent grey-ramp steps differ by more than four times the largest adjacent difference at default; on the grey ramp, Split toning keeps lightness in order, and Duotone's output lightness runs in one direction (darker to lighter, or the reverse when the shadows colour is the lighter one). The 8-bit preview and the frame the export encodes stay within ΔE00 1 of the full-precision result.<br>• Colour pop, Split toning and the mixer's Hue and Saturation keep lightness (*Measures*).<br>• The exported file matches the preview within NFR-08 with every colour tool on.<br>*Reset, copy and compare:* each slider has a per-row Reset, as in RULE-08, and bipolar sliders have its drag detent at 0. Each tool has a Reset that returns all its settings to default. Reset all (RULE-08) also returns every colour tool to default; the look is kept. Apply to all, Copy and Paste carry the colour tools with the adjustments and look. Compare shows the clip without adjustments, colour tools and look.<br>*History:* each slider movement, choice of a setting (a kept colour, a duotone pair), swap, pick, removal of a replacement or reset is one undo step (RULE-13). Opening or closing a tool's group is not, and neither is choosing which colour range the mixer shows: that choice is not stored in the clip and is kept for the session, like open groups. |
| **RULE-25** | **Colour replace** (video and photo clips, D-053).<br>*Count:* up to 3 replacements per clip, none by default. Adding a fourth is unavailable, with COPY-replace.limit.<br>*Each replacement:* a From colour and a To colour (sRGB #RRGGBB); Range 0 to 100, default 30; Softness 0 to 100, default 50. Adding one starts pick mode; a successful pick adds the replacement with From and To both set to the picked colour, so it changes nothing until To is changed: a replacement whose To equals its From is skipped (it takes no share under *Several replacements*). Picking again for an existing replacement sets its From only; To is kept. Cancelling adds or changes nothing. A replacement can be removed.<br>*Matching:* each pixel gets a match weight from 0 to 1 by how close its hue is to the From colour's (RULE-24 *Measures*); its lightness is ignored, so lit and shaded parts of one colour both match. The weight is 1 within the *full distance* either side of From's hue, 5° + 0.55° × Range (5° at 0, 21.5° at 30, 60° at 100), and fades smoothly to 0 over a further *fade distance*, 2° + (full distance − 2°) × Softness ÷ 100 (11.75° at the defaults). It is multiplied by a chroma fade, smooth from 1 at half of From's chroma to 0 at a quarter of it, and by RULE-24's grey fade, so grey pixels never match. Matching is by colour, not by object: everything of that colour in the clip changes.<br>*Change:* each replacement has one turning direction: the shorter way from the From colour's hue to the To colour's (increasing hue when they are exactly opposite). A pixel with match weight *w* has its hue turned *w* of the way to the To colour's hue in that direction, its own distance from From's hue being counted on its side of From, so neighbouring pixels turn by nearly the same angle and full matches land exactly on To's hue; and its chroma multiplied by (1 − *w*) + *w* × (To chroma ÷ From chroma). Hue and chroma are the working space's (RULE-24 *Measures*). Its lightness is kept, so shading and texture stay. With a grey To colour the hue is not moved and full matches become grey. A result beyond what the output can show loses chroma, keeping hue and lightness.<br>*Several replacements:* all match weights are computed on the picture entering colour replace. Each later replacement acts only on the share the earlier ones left: its effective weight is its own weight × (1 − the sum of the earlier effective weights), so a pixel's total never exceeds 1 and a pixel the first matches in full takes only the first's change. Changes apply in list order, each with its effective weight.<br>*Grey and faint From colours:* a From colour with C* below 2, picked or entered, is refused with COPY-notice.replace_grey; From stays as it was, and with Add nothing is added. A From colour with C* from 2 to below 12 is accepted: its matches lie in RULE-24's grey fade, so they change only in part, and COPY-notice.replace_faint is shown once for that pick or entry.<br>*Pick mode:* entered with Add colour to replace or Pick from preview. Playback pauses. If the playhead is on the selected clip it stays; otherwise it moves to the clip's first frame at or after the end of its fade in and of any cross-fade into it, or to the clip's last frame if that is earlier (not an undo step). While pick mode is on, the preview shows only the selected clip's source picture, placed in the frame over the background (a Blur background is made from that same ungraded source picture), without adjustments, colour tools, look, fades, the other clip of a cross-fade or text layers: exactly the picture that is sampled. Leaving pick mode restores the preview. Dragging or pinching on the preview never moves or scales the clip. The frame-step controls stay available; pick mode ends if the playhead leaves the selected clip. Play ends pick mode without a change, then plays. Pick mode also ends after a successful pick, on Esc or Cancel, when the selection changes, on entering full screen, on New project and when an export starts.<br>*Picking:* a click or tap on the clip's picture (with a mouse, on release) sets From to the colour of the source picture there: averaged in linear light over a disc of radius 1 % of `s`, counting only the clip's own picture, rounded to #RRGGBB, then tested for grey. The clip's picture is the placed clip inside the frame; the background (colour or Blur) is outside it. On touch, pressing and dragging shows a magnifier: an enlarged view of the picture around the finger with a centre ring in the colour a lift would pick, above the finger, or below it near the preview's top edge; lifting picks. With a keyboard, a marker starts at the centre of the part of the clip's picture inside the frame; Left and Right move it by 1 % of the frame's width, Up and Down by 1 % of its height (Shift: 10 %), kept within that part; its value reads COPY-replace.marker_value; Enter or Space picks, Esc cancels. A grey pick, or a click, tap or lift outside the clip's picture, changes nothing and keeps pick mode on (a grey pick shows COPY-notice.replace_grey), so the user can pick again. A successful pick is one undo step, announced with COPY-replace.picked. From can also be typed or chosen with the colour picker. |
